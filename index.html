Jules Jacobs

Assistant Professor of Computer Science, ETH Zürich

I work on programming languages and formal verification: type systems, program logics and automated verifiers for systems code, concurrent programs, computer networks and probabilistic programs. I'm looking for PhD students.

Before ETH, I was a visiting researcher at Jane Street and a postdoc at Cornell with Alexandra Silva and Nate Foster, and did my PhD at Radboud with Robbert Krebbers and Stephanie Balzer.

Some of my favourite things are: type theory, parametricity, SSA as a dataflow lattice, generating functions & Kirchhoff's matrix-tree theorem, differential forms & the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, star semirings, Lagrangian mechanics.

News: a preprint on dataflow analysis by pattern matching, with a video that explains it · papers on mode crossing in OxCaml (ICFP'26), exact inference for probabilistic programs (OOPSLA'26), network-enforced session types (ECOOP'26) and deciding serializability (TACAS'26) · the IPA Dissertation Award for my thesis · teaching the Software Engineering Seminar at ETH.

Research projects

Safe and verified systems programming

The goal is systems code that is fast, safe, and checked against its specification. As AI agents write more of our code, checked specifications let people stay in charge of what that code does.

  • Mode Crossing ICFP'26talk recordingcodeLets OxCaml strengthen the modes of values automatically when their type makes it safe, as for immutable data.
  • Stable patterns preprintvideodemoOne analysis that finds what constant propagation, unboxing and closure analysis find separately.
  • Vox toolslidescodeChecks specifications at compile time on top of OxCaml, with refinement types and ghost proofs that are erased before code generation.
  • OxCaml languageplaygroundcourseJane Street's extension of OCaml for safe, fast systems programming; its modes track whether a value can escape its stack frame or be shared between threads.
  • Eggstract toolExtracts shared, acyclic DAGs from e-graphs, with a Rust library, a command-line tool and an optional CP-SAT backend.

CollaboratorsBenjamin Peters, Diana Kalinichenko, Liam Stevenson, Aspen Smith, Derek Dreyer, Richard A. Eisenberg and Fabian Lukas Grubmüller

Network verification and automata

We build verifiers that answer questions about networks, such as reachability and isolation, typically in well under a second, and the automata algorithms that they rest on.

  • KATch PLDI'24playgroundslidestalk recordingcodeChecks NetKAT network specifications for properties like reachability and isolation, often in under a second.
  • StacKAT PLDI'25videodemoslidestalk recordingcodeExtends NetKAT with a packet stack, with a decision procedure and complete axioms for program equivalence.
  • NEST ECOOP'26codeEnforces application protocols inside the network, with packet-level monitors generated from session types.
  • SER TACAS'26codeDecides whether programs in the SER language are serializable, giving a certificate or a counterexample.
  • Boa POPL'23slidestalk recordingcodeOne generic algorithm that minimizes many kinds of automata up to bisimilarity, also on large systems.
  • Petra toolA Petri-net reachability solver.

CollaboratorsNate Foster, Alexandra Silva, Jana Wagemaker, Guy Amir, Mark Moeller, Tobias Kappé, Dexter Kozen, Lily Saada, Ernest Ng, Jens Kanstrup Larsen, Alceste Scalas, Mark Barbone, Nicolas Amat, Olivier Savary Belanger, David Darais, Cole Schlesinger, Steffen Smolka and Thorsten Wißmann

Deadlock freedom and verified concurrency

Programs that communicate over channels or share locks can deadlock. We design type systems and separation logics in which well-typed or verified programs never deadlock, and later work also verifies that programs follow their protocols and compute the right results. All proofs are mechanized in Coq/Rocq using Iris.

  • Connectivity graphs POPL'22slidestalk recordingcodeA method, mechanized in Coq, for proving that concurrent languages are free of deadlocks and memory leaks.
  • Multiparty GV ICFP'22slidestalk recordingcodeA functional language with multiparty session types in which well-typed programs are deadlock free.
  • Self-dual session types ECOOP'22slidestalk recordingcodeAdds only a fork construct to linear λ-calculus: enough to encode session types, and deadlock free.
  • Locks POPL'23slidestalk recordingcodeA λ-calculus with higher-order locks in which well-typed programs cannot deadlock or leak memory.
  • Session protocols ICFP'23slidestalk recordingcodeBuilds dependent session protocols in separation logic on one-shot channels, in under 1000 lines of Coq.
  • Deadlock-free separation logic POPL'24slidestalk recordingcodeA separation logic for message passing in which deadlock and leak freedom follow from linearity alone.
  • Multris OOPSLA'24slidescodeA separation logic for proving programs correct that mix multiparty message passing with shared memory.
  • PhD thesis 2024talk recordingcodeLanguages and logics in which deadlock freedom, leak freedom and other guarantees follow by construction.

CollaboratorsRobbert Krebbers, Stephanie Balzer and Jonas Kastberg Hinrichsen

Probabilistic programming

What a probabilistic program means, and how to compute its distribution exactly.

  • Slice OOPSLA'26slidescodeRewrites continuous probabilistic programs into equivalent discrete ones that exact inference engines can run.
  • Paradoxes POPL'21videoslidestalk recordingcodeRemoves paradoxes of conditioning on measure-zero events by tracking infinitesimal probabilities.

CollaboratorsKatherine Wu, Kevin Batz and Alexandra Silva

Some of this work has found its way into industry:

  • Jane Street. I work with Jane Street on OxCaml. Mode crossing is implemented in the OxCaml compiler and deployed in Jane Street's code base, and Vox builds compile-time verification on top of OxCaml.
  • Google. Google's open-source NetKAT implementation builds its packet sets and transformers on our verifier KATch.

If your company has software or infrastructure where stronger guarantees would help, I'd be glad to talk about working together, for example on a joint project or by hosting one of my students as an intern. Email me.

Students

Current PhD students. Fabian Lukas Grubmüller.

Prospective PhD students. Want to build proof assistants, verifiers, type systems or programming languages as a PhD student? Email me first, with [PhD] in the subject, your CV, a few lines about what you would like to work on and why, and one thing you have made or done that you are proud of: a program, a proof, a project or a piece of writing, with a link if there is one. The formal application goes through the department's doctoral program, or through the Direct Doctorate if you have a bachelor's degree.

Master's theses and semester projects. If you are a student at ETH, I supervise theses and projects in these areas. Email me with [Thesis] in the subject.

A strong mathematical background helps, as does experience with proof assistants, compilers or verification tools.
